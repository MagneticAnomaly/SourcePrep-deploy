 the most professional and scalable approach—especially when you want to remain anonymous—is the **"Publisher" or "Holdco" (Holding Company) model**.

In this model, your website acts as the "corporate HQ" that establishes trust and lists your products, while the apps themselves take center stage.

Here is the recommended structure and content for your anonymous LLC website.

### 1. Site Structure (The "Publisher" Model)
Keep the navigation dead simple. You only need 4–5 core pages/sections.
*   **Home / Portfolio:** The main showcase.
*   **About / Mission:** Your philosophy (without your face).
*   **Support:** A central hub for user help.
*   **Press:** Assets for journalists/bloggers.
*   **Legal:** Privacy Policy & Terms.

---

### 2. What to Put on Each Page

#### **A. Home Page (The Showcase)**
Since you want to "simply showcase the apps," this should be your homepage. Don't bury the apps behind a "Welcome" screen.
*   **Hero Section:** A one-sentence tagline about what connects your apps (e.g., *"Simple productivity tools for busy professionals"* or *"Privacy-focused utilities for iOS"*).
*   **App Grid:** Large, high-quality icons or device mockups for each app.
*   **Direct Call-to-Action (CTA):** "Download on the App Store" badges immediately visible for each app.
*   **One-Line Pitch:** Under each app, write one clear sentence about what problem it solves.

#### **B. The "About" Page (The Anonymity Shield)**
This is where most people put bios. Since you want to be anonymous, **replace "Who We Are" with "What We Believe."**
*   **Mission Statement:** Focus on the *ethos* of the company.
    *   *Example:* "We build software that respects your time and data. We believe in tools that do one thing well, without the bloat."
*   **Origin Story (Depersonalized):** You can tell the story of *why* the apps were built without saying *who* built them.
    *   *Example:* "XYZ LLC was founded in 2024 to solve the problem of complex mobile tracking..."
*   **Values:** List 3 core values (e.g., Privacy First, Ad-Free, User-Centric). This builds trust more effectively than a photo of a developer.

#### **C. Centralized Support Hub**
Apple and Google require a support URL. Hosting this on your LLC site saves you from managing separate support sites for every new app.
*   **FAQ:** A simple accordion list of common questions for *all* your apps.
*   **Contact Form:** Do **not** put your email address (e.g., `name@company.com`) directly on the site. Use a contact form or a generic alias (e.g., `support@company.com`).
*   **Ticketing System:** If you want to be extra professional, link this form to a helpdesk tool (like Freshdesk or HelpScout). It looks bigger than a one-person shop and hides your identity.

#### **D. Press Kit (Essential for Growth)**
Journalists and bloggers appreciate this. If they have to email you to get a logo, they won't write about you.
*   **Assets:** High-resolution app icons, screenshots, and feature graphics.
*   **Copy:** A "boileplate" description of the company and each app (50-100 words) that they can copy-paste into articles.
*   **Media Contact:** A specific generic email for press inquiries (e.g., `press@company.com`).

#### **E. Legal Footer**
*   **Privacy Policy:** Essential for App Store approval. You can have one "Master Policy" that covers all apps if the data handling is similar, or link to specific ones.
*   **Terms of Service:** Standard legal protection for your LLC.
*   **Business Address:** **Tip:** Do not use your home address. Use a Virtual Mailbox or the Registered Agent address if your state allows it to serve as a contact address, to maintain privacy.

---

### 3. How to Build Trust Without a "Face"
When users can't see a human, they judge the company by the quality of the assets.
*   **Design Quality:** Minimalism is your friend. A clean, fast-loading site (black and white, high-contrast) looks like a serious software studio. (Check out *Panic* or *Tapbots* for inspiration—though they have bios, their design style is "product-first").
*   **Writing Voice:** Use "We" instead of "I". It implies a team and infrastructure, even if it's just you.
*   **Social Proof:** If you have any reviews, even anonymous App Store reviews, feature them. "5 Stars on the App Store" is a powerful trust signal that requires no names.

### Summary Checklist
*   [ ] **Domain:** `YourLLCName.com`
*   [ ] **Email:** Setup `support@...` and `hello@...` (forwarded to you).
*   [ ] **Content:** High-res screenshots, "Mission" copy, Privacy Policy.
*   [ ] **Anonymity Check:** Ensure your domain WHOIS is set to "Private" (usually free with most registrars now).

This structure makes you look like a "Publishing House" rather than a freelancer, which increases the perceived value of your apps while keeping your personal identity completely private.
